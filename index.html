HELPFUL TO KNOW

Before you begin.

Practical answers for product orders, custom development and collaborative research.

How do I order a product or start a custom project?

For an antibody or LFA test, share the target or product name, quantity and intended application. For a custom project, outline your research question, target, species and desired outcome. Use the enquiry form or email usaurabh@illinois.edu to discuss availability, ordering or a project quotation.

How do I choose the right antibody for my application?

Review the target, clone ID, species reactivity, tested applications, product format and available quality-control documentation. Share your planned method and sample context if you need help selecting a reagent. You can begin with our Influenza A antibody information.

How much do products and services cost?

The LFA tests shown on this site are $5 per test. Standard mAb development starts at $11,000 per antigen for academic projects and $14,000 per antigen for industry projects. Custom assays, diagnostic testing, bioinformatics and preclinical research are quoted according to scope. See mAb pricing and included services for details.

What is the expected turnaround time?

Product delivery depends on availability and order quantity. Custom-project timelines depend on starting materials, screening requirements, assay complexity and study scope. Include your preferred deadline when enquiring so availability, milestones and an expected timeline can be discussed with your quotation.

Can I supply my own antigen or an existing hybridoma?

Yes. Standard mAb development uses a suitable customer-supplied protein antigen, and an existing hybridoma can be considered for production and purification. Share the material identity, available quantity, concentration, buffer and any existing characterization so the team can assess the starting point and proposed work.

Which diagnostic test formats can I discuss with the team?

The Hub covers ELISA, PCR, qRT-PCR, lateral flow tests and multiplex immunoassays. The appropriate format depends on the target, species, sample type and intended application. You can enquire about an existing test, bring an assay prototype or discuss development of a new format.

What samples should I provide, and how should I send them?

Sample type, minimum amount, handling and transport requirements depend on the test or project. Please obtain the agreed submission instructions and shipping destination from the responsible team before dispatch. Include the species, target, specimen type and sample count in your initial enquiry.

Can you help analyze sequencing data I already have?

Share the organism, sequencing platform, data format, number of samples, experimental design and analysis question. The relevant team can discuss an analysis plan, required metadata, deliverables and a file-transfer method. Keep a copy of your original data; the enquiry form is for initial project details rather than large-file uploads.

How do I begin an animal-model or preclinical research project?

Start with the research question, study objectives, desired readouts, candidate technology and preferred timeline. A planning discussion with the responsible research team helps identify an appropriate model, sample strategy and feasible project scope.

What will I receive at the end of a project?

The written project scope defines the deliverables and reporting format. Depending on the service, these can include antibody preparations and clone documentation, assay results, analysis reports or agreed preclinical study readouts. Discuss any specific data files, documentation or material quantities you need before the work begins.

How are material transfer, ownership and commercial use handled?

Include your intended commercial use and any material-transfer needs in the initial discussion. Applicable written agreements define material use, ownership, confidentiality and other project terms. The Illinois Office of Technology Management manages outbound material-transfer agreements and provides institutional guidance for researchers and commercial partners.

Where should I request routine clinical diagnostic testing?

For routine clinical specimen submission and established veterinary diagnostic testing, use the Illinois Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory. The Hub enquiry route supports its reagent products, technology development, specialized project discussions and research services.
